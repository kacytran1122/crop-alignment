\section{Threats to validity and evidence inventory}

\paragraph{The diagnostic has a narrow domain.} Crop alignment is defined only on
pixels for which overlapping crops receive different labels. If that set is empty,
there are no observations from which to estimate $\kap$. The method therefore
cannot audit crop-invariant pseudo-label shortcuts or shortcuts unrelated to crop
geometry. It would be incorrect to turn an empty set into a zero and call it a
negative result.

\paragraph{The contrast is not unique causal attribution.} A nonzero $\kap$ proves
that a predictor changes with crop context on the disagreement set. The matched
control removes the crop sensitivity learned under repaired labels, but it cannot
guarantee that the only remaining treatment--control difference is imitation of the
labeller. The experiments support a targeted diagnostic claim, not a mediation
claim about a unique learning mechanism.

\paragraph{The release has three evidence tiers.} The primary sea-ice aggregate is
recomputed from 16 paired values in committed JSON. Flood calibration and cost rows,
and the 60/120-epoch sea-ice results, are committed as summary logs. Raw predictions,
checkpoints, and some per-run JSON are absent. Thus the compact artifact verifies
aggregate arithmetic but cannot regenerate every prediction or training run. A
seventeenth rounded sea-ice row without matching JSON is excluded from the primary
claim.

\section{Errors in our own analysis}
\label{sec:errors}

The main paper discloses the artifact-provenance failure that changes its headline
sample. This appendix records that issue and the rest of the analysis audit. We
report them for the same reason we report the benchmark audit: an error list that
only contains errors which turned out not to matter is not an error list.

Two patterns account for most of them. The first is that a wrong number is more
dangerous than a crash, because a crash stops the pipeline and a wrong number
propagates. The second is narrower and worse: a check that reports success over a
failure. Five of the entries below are of that kind, marked \textbf{[silent]}. None of
the five raised, and all five would have passed unnoticed.

\subsection{Errors that changed a number we had written down}

\paragraph{1. The lead statistic was the wrong statistic.} An earlier draft
contrasted a spread of $0.1164$ against seed variance. That figure is the
\emph{unpaired} standard deviation of levels, and every comparison it was supporting
is paired, both arms on the same held-out acquisition, so acquisition difficulty
cancels. The correct framing is a minimum detectable effect from the paired spread.
The conclusion survived; the statistic did not.

\paragraph{2. A fold exists only in a rounded text summary.}
The committed JSON contains 16 aligned noisy/control folds and recomputes to
$+0.1073$ ($t=12.88$). A text summary contains a seventeenth row and reports
$+0.1106$ ($t=13.01$), but the corresponding per-fold machine-readable output is
absent. An earlier draft promoted the text-only aggregate. The main paper now uses
only the 16-fold result and discloses the gap; a larger number is not evidence when
the release cannot reconstruct it.

\paragraph{3. Significance computed after averaging away the variance.} Gate 3
averaged confusion matrices across seeds and then bootstrapped, which removes seed
variance from the interval. Recomputed as paired differences across the five seeds,
none of the three contrasts is significant. Two claims stated as significant were
withdrawn (\Cref{sec:withdrawn}).

\paragraph{4. A $7.2\times$ contradiction between adjacent sections.} The deficit at
two acquisitions appeared as $-0.0295$ from the controlled learning curve and
$-0.2121$ from a different table. They are not two estimates of one quantity: they
differ in data, in scoring convention, and in training budget. Presented on one axis
they implied a disagreement that did not exist.

\paragraph{5. Three spreads for one quantity.} The 12-epoch premium is recorded with
$s = 0.0859$ in one runner header, $0.0910$ in the paper, and an implied $0.1003$
behind a required-$n$ of 238 quoted in the same header. The current computation gives
$0.0910$ and 197 reproducibly. The header predates a fix and was never recomputed.

\paragraph{6. A hand-picked subset described as balanced.} The runner for the
six-fold budget preview records its folds as ``the two largest, the two smallest, and
the one negative fold.'' The benchmark has three negative folds. The preview included
one, and excluded the largest fold, both of the two smallest positives, and the
acquisition that later turns out to move most between training budgets. We had
recorded the preview's failure as an error of subset size. It was a selection rule
that was never checked against the data it claimed to describe.

\paragraph{7. Bonferroni tolerance overstated.} A claim that every headline result
survives a Bonferroni factor of 100 was true for two of three. The accuracy cost at
$t = -4.27$ is $p = 0.0016$ on 10 degrees of freedom and tolerates a factor of about
30. The main paper now states the weakest case rather than the strongest.

\paragraph{8. A coefficient tested against the wrong reference.} The threshold-arm
coefficient $\beta$ was tested against $0$, which it obviously differs from. The
meaningful reference is $\beta = 1$, the value the difference in differences assumes.
Against that reference $\hat{\beta} = 0.711$ is not significant, which is the honest
reading and the one the paper reports.

\paragraph{9. Independence claimed for nested settings.} Five dial settings were
described as five independent tests. The dial nests them by construction and they
share the same eleven events, so what they show is consistency across a sweep, not
five separate results.

\subsection{Checks that reported success over a failure}

\paragraph{10. A convergence test nothing could satisfy \textbf{[silent]}.}
\texttt{epoch\_control.py} asked whether \texttt{best\_epoch >= 60} for runs capped at
60 epochs. No run can satisfy that, so the script printed ``every long run converged
before the cap'' over best epochs of 59, 56 and 51. The criterion that carries meaning
is whether early stopping ever fired.

\paragraph{11. An octal literal that killed 33 of 55 jobs \textbf{[silent]}.} A sweep
derived its parameter with \texttt{python -c "print(\$\{A\}/100.0)"} on a zero-padded
tag. Python rejects \texttt{050} as a malformed octal literal, while \texttt{000} and
\texttt{100} are both legal. Exactly the two ends of the sweep survived and all 33
interior jobs died, and the loop announced completion with 22 of 55 results on disk.
Running the sweep ends-first is the only reason this cost jobs rather than the claim.

\paragraph{12. A type coercion that ate an underscore \textbf{[silent]}.}
\texttt{pd.read\_json} coerced acquisition identifiers to \texttt{int64}, so a drift
test matched nothing, reported ``0 complete folds'' and exited 0.

\paragraph{13. A layout check that could not match its own pattern \textbf{[silent]}.}
Overfull lines were counted with a shell command in which the doubled backslash was
literal, so it reported zero while three tables overflowed their column. The error was
found from a rendered page image, not from the log.

\paragraph{14. A negative control that tested nothing \textbf{[silent]}.} To confirm
the layout check could detect a fault, a deliberate break was introduced with a
substitution whose pattern never matched. Nothing was broken, the check passed, and
the pass was recorded as evidence that the check worked.

\subsection{Errors that a build or a log would not report}

\paragraph{15--16. Two control characters from shell quoting.} \verb|\times| became a
tab, rendering ``2.7imes'' in the compiled PDF; \verb|\approx| became a BEL byte.
Neither produced a build error and both exit 0. Both were introduced by escaping a
LaTeX macro through a shell string, and both were found by scanning the source bytes
rather than reading the log.

\paragraph{17. A figure that contradicted its own text.} The main text declines to
quote a required sample size for crop alignment, because the power formula returns a
value below two and a paired test cannot estimate a variance from one fold. The figure
drew that same quantity as a bar labelled ``1''.

\paragraph{18. Four floats never referenced.} LaTeX does not warn about a table or
figure that no text points at. Four were unreferenced, which also degraded their
placement.

\paragraph{19--20. A renumbering trap, twice.} A cross-reference to a section that did
not yet exist has no heading to anchor against, so a renumbering pass moved it to a
different section. The dangling-reference check passed both times, because the wrong
target exists.

\section{Claims withdrawn from an earlier version}
\label{sec:withdrawn}

\paragraph{Gate 3 significance.} ``Every difference is significant under the paired
bootstrap'' and ``the true-photon arm is significantly worse than a constant'' were
both stated in an earlier version. Recomputed at the run level across five seeds,
neither holds. Withdrawn.

\paragraph{The seed-variance framing.} An earlier version argued that acquisition
spread exceeded seed variance using an unpaired standard deviation of levels.
Recomputed as a paired quantity, seed variance is $0.31\%$ of the premium variance,
which is a stronger statement than the one withdrawn, but the original argument was
not evidence for it.

\paragraph{``The leak is eliminated.''} Three nulls were accepted at $n = 3$ with a
spread near $0.07$, where an effect of $0.03$ would have been undetectable.
``Eliminated'' overstated ``not detected''. Because the release contains no
independently justified equivalence margin, the revised paper treats these as
underpowered absence-of-evidence statements and does not use them to claim
elimination.

\paragraph{A prediction we made and lost.} We derived a point prediction for the
patch-versus-mosaic gap with nothing fitted. The intercept lands where theory puts it
and the sign agrees on 14 of 17 folds, but the magnitude is out by a factor of 1.6 to
1.9. The prediction is reported in the main paper as made and refused, rather than
removed.
